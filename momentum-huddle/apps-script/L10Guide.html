Your Guide to the Momentum Huddle

Welcome to the Paid Media team. This is our weekly team meeting — what it is, how it runs front-to-back, what you own in it, and what to expect.

Tuesdays · 10:00 · ~60–90 min · run from the Momentum Huddle app in the team workbook
The big idea. Every week the same short agenda, run on a timer. We read the numbers, check our priorities, and spend most of the hour solving real problems for good — not just talking about them. The meeting earns its name when the team rates it a 10/10 on the way out.

Same seven segments every week. The app runs a countdown for each, so we stay tight. Here's the shape of the hour:

6 · Solve — the real meeting

Each block ≈ its minutes. Solve (solving issues) is most of the meeting on purpose. The classic format is 90 min; we trim to fit our slot.
1 · Check-in
5 min
One personal + one business good-news item from each person. Kudos and shout-outs welcome.
You: share one personal win and one work win. Give a teammate a shout-out if they earned one.
2 · Metrics
5 min
We read the weekly numbers. Each metric is simply on track or off track — no explaining or solving here. Off track? It's sent to Issues for later.
You: call your own metrics on/off track in one word. Don't defend — just flag it.
3 · Priority review
5 min
"Priorities" are the quarter's big goals. Each owner says on track / off track. Same no-discussion rule; off track is sent to Issues.
You: report your priorities' status honestly. Tick off any milestones you completed.
4 · Headlines
5 min
Quick one-liners: customer news, people news, FYIs. Flag anything worth passing up the chain.
You: drop any headline you know about — a customer comment, a competitor move, a kudos.
5 · To-do list
5 min
Last week's to-dos: done or not done — nothing in between. Not-done carries over and gets visibly stale. Team target is ≥ 90% done.
You: mark your to-dos done/not done truthfully. The score is the team's, not yours alone.
6 · Solve
60 min
The real meeting. We vote the issues list, take the top one, and run it I → D → D: Identify the true root cause, Discuss it once, Decide it. The solution becomes a to-do with one owner and a date. Solve it forever, not just for this week.
You: raise issues (even half-formed). Vote. Help find root causes. Take an owner role when it's yours.
7 · Wrap-up
5 min
Recap new to-dos, send the recap, and everyone rates the meeting 1–10. Below an 8: say where it lost you.
You: rate honestly. If it wasn't a 10, name the one thing that would've made it one.

5What you own

As an analyst on the team, four things in the huddle are yours:

Metrics

The weekly numbers for your accounts. You report them on/off track each week. They mostly auto-fill — your job is to know them and react.

Priorities

Your 1–3 big priorities for the quarter, broken into dated milestones. You drive them and report progress. Finish all milestones → the priority is done.

To-dos

The small commitments you take on each week (often from a solved issue). One owner, one date, done within ~7 days.

Issues

Problems, blockers, ideas. Raise them freely — in the email or in the room. The team solves the top ones together in Solve.

6What to do — before, during, after

Before (Mon–Tue AM)

  • Read the Monday heads-up email
  • Finish (or update) your open to-dos
  • Reply with any issues / headlines / priorities
  • Know your metrics

During the huddle

  • Share a check-in + any kudos
  • Call your metrics & priorities on/off track
  • Mark to-dos done / not done honestly
  • Raise & vote on issues; help solve them
  • Rate the meeting 1–10

After (the week)

  • Read the recap email
  • Do the to-dos you took on
  • Move your priority milestones forward
  • Park bigger 1:1 topics for your 1:1

7What to expect (the house rules)

On track or off track — that's it.
In Metrics & Priority review there's no explaining or solving. Off track just gets sent to Issues. Keeps us fast.
Solve forever, not for this week.
Solve digs for the root cause so the same issue doesn't come back. A quick patch isn't the goal.
Bring problems, not polished answers.
You don't need a solution to raise an issue. Half-formed is welcome — that's what the room is for.
The to-do score is the team's.
The ≥90% completion target is measured for the team together, not person-by-person. Help each other clear the list.
Rate it honestly.
Low ratings make the meeting better. If it wasn't a 10, say what would've made it one — no hard feelings.
Your first few weeks.
Mostly observe, share a check-in, and ask questions. You'll pick up metrics and priorities as your accounts are handed over.

8Quick glossary

Check-inThe opening round of personal + business good news. A deliberate human warm-up before work talk.
MetricsThe handful of weekly numbers we watch. Read as on/off track only.
PriorityA big priority for the quarter (the big items you fit in first). Owned by one person, broken into milestones.
MilestoneA dated checkpoint inside a priority. Tick them off as you go; the last one done finishes the priority.
HeadlineA short FYI — customer, employee, or kudos news — worth the team knowing.
To-doA 7-day commitment with one owner and a date. Usually born from solving an issue.
IssueAnything to work through: a problem, blocker, or idea. The raw material for Solve.
SolveIdentify → Discuss → Decide. How we take an issue to a real, lasting fix.
ParkSending an issue to a smaller meeting (like a 1:1) when it doesn't belong in the team room.
CascadeA note that a headline or decision should be passed on to a wider meeting.
