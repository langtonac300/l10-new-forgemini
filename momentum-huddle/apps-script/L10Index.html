How a Momentum Huddle runs

Same agenda every week, same order, hard timeboxes. The meeting earns its keep when the team rates it 10/10 on the way out.

The agenda (default 90 minutes — segment lengths are editable in L10_Config)

SegmentMinWhat happens — and what doesn't
Check-in5One personal + one business good-news item each. Connect as people; no work talk yet. Kudos shout-outs land here.
Metrics5Read the weekly numbers: each is on track or off track — nothing else. Off track? Say "send it to Issues". No discussing, no explaining, no solving here.
Priority review5Each priority's owner says "on track" or "off track". Off track is sent to Issues. Same rule: no discussion. Each priority's milestones sit on a to-scale timeline under it — tick them off as they land; the last one marks the priority done.
Headlines5One-liners: customer news, people news, FYIs. Anything that needs talk is sent to Issues.
To-do list5Read last week's to-dos: done or not done. Team target ≥90% done. Not-done items carry over (and get visibly stale).
Solve60The real meeting. Pick the top issues by vote, then for each: Identify the root cause, Discuss it once (no politicking, no re-runs), Decide it — the solution becomes a to-do with one owner and a date. Solve for good, not for this week.
Wrap-up5Recap new to-dos, pick what cascades to leadership, everyone rates the meeting 1–10. Below 8: say where it lost you.

House rules

  • The metrics never get discussed in the metrics segment. "Send it to Issues" is the whole conversation. That's what keeps 5 minutes at 5 minutes.
  • One owner per to-do. "We'll all look at it" means nobody will.
  • Issues are not status updates. If it doesn't need a decision or a solution, it's a headline.
  • Park what belongs in a 1:1. Two-person topics don't spend the whole room's hour — park the issue to the right 1:1 and move on.
  • Numbers carry their caveats. A metric can show a data caveat — read the number with it.
  • Solved means written down. The resolution gets logged on the issue; the actions get owners and dates. Otherwise it comes back in a month wearing a different hat.

Where everything lives

This app reads and writes the L10_* tabs in this spreadsheet — they are the database and the audit trail. Edit definitions there (metrics, config, events); run the meeting here.

The Forge page is a different kind of meeting: a timed, phone-and-projector idea-generation session (private writing, relay, blind votes, a goal form, peer review, then Lock) used for goal-setting days and quarterly priority resets. It writes to the L10_Forge_* and L10_Goals tabs and, at Lock, into each person's own goals tab and the priorities list — never into to-dos.

The Strategy page is deliberately not on the agenda: it holds the cross-account initiatives the strategist works between huddles (L10_Initiatives and its matrix / trail tabs). What it spawns — to-dos, hub tests, priorities — shows up here through the normal doors.

Issue forms match what you type against your analysis playbook (L10_Playbook) and past decisions, so recurring problems start from the record instead of memory. The Wrap-up segment circles back on solved issues to ask whether the fix held — that verdict builds the decision ledger on the History page.

A team-wide initiative, idea to rollout

  1. Courtney and Alex shape it: the idea, the test and what success looks like.
  2. Courtney proves it: runs the test, usually through CJ on a Brady account.
  3. Alex approves the rollout: which accounts, in what order and how it ranks against current work.
  4. Each account owner applies it: they decide how it fits their account. If it doesn't fit, they take the reason to Alex, who makes the call.
  5. Results go back to Courtney: she updates the playbook. Initiatives are tracked on the Strategy page.
